# Historical sources

Every fact placed in `Assets/Data/*.json` must be traceable to an entry here. Format: what was used, where, link, date accessed.

- (Phase 0) `figures.json` carried over from the Three.js prototype; personalities and goals are dramatised from general knowledge of the Anarchy (Gesta Stephani; William of Malmesbury, *Historia Novella*; Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, Peterborough continuation). To be re-verified and cited properly in Phase 3.
- (Phase 1) Coastline of Great Britain: `datasets/geo-countries` (https://github.com/datasets/geo-countries, countries.geojson, feature GBR), derived from Natural Earth, public domain. Accessed 2026-11-07. Processed into `UnityProject/Assets/Data/england_coast.json`.
- (Phase 1) City coordinates (London, Winchester, Oxford, Wallingford, Bristol, Gloucester, Lincoln) from standard gazetteer latitude/longitude; upland and river positions placed by hand from a physical map of England and Wales and scaled stylistically.
- (Phase 1) Start date: Stephen was crowned at Westminster on 22 December 1135, a Sunday (Julian calendar). Canonical hours (Prime, Terce, Sext, None, Vespers, Compline) are the monastic day offices; parish bells rang fewer and less regular hours, a simplification noted for Phase 4's village day.
- (Phase 16) Wallingford, for `Sim/TownPlan.cs` (the plan is code, not JSON, so the figures sit beside their tests). Accessed 2026-11-12.
  - Reading Abbey study notes, "Wallingford" (http://readingabbey.org.uk/pdf-files/wallingfordnotes.pdf): defensive zone of 43 hectares; earthen ramparts with timber palisade and water-filled ditch; streets dividing four quarters; castle begun c. 1070 in the north-east; priory in the north-west; north-west (Bullcroft) and south-west (Kinecroft) left open; "population perhaps 2,000 in c 500 houses"; 22 Frenchmen's dwellings; eleven parochial churches and three chapels in the twelfth century; shire centre with mint; weekly market; Stephen's siege castles at Crowmarsh.
  - N. Christie and others, "Sources, Results and Questions" (Wallingford Burh to Borough Research Project), Oxoniensia 2010 (https://www.oxoniensia.org/volumes/2010/Christie.pdf): rampart up to 7 m from bank top to ditch bottom; Holy Trinity priory at the south end of the Bullcroft; the Kinecroft open but with short-lived twelfth-century housing; names of the medieval churches (St Peter, St Martin, St Mary the Less, St John, St Mary the More, St Michael, St Rumbold, St Lucian, All Saints, Holy Trinity, St Leonard); St Mary-le-More as the market church; churches on or near the defences; timber, cob and chalk buildings in excavation.
  - D. Roffe, "Wallingford in Domesday Book" (https://www.roffe.co.uk/wallingford1.htm), read from the search excerpt only: "the 1086 total of probably 400 or more properties".
  - Wikipedia, "Wallingford Castle" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallingford_Castle): built 1067 to 1071, probably by Robert D'Oyly; motte 60 m across and 13 m high; strengthened in stone by Brien FitzCount, probably in the 1130s; sieges of 1139, 1146 and 1152 to 1153; knights billeted in town houses; the prison.
  - Not sourced, and marked as estimates in the code: the exact line of every lane, the sites of the lost churches (only St Mary-le-More at the market, Holy Trinity at the priory, St Martin at the crossroads, St Peter by the bridge, St Leonard in the south-east and St Lucian outside the South Gate follow the record), who lived on which plot, and the shares of the trades, which follow the general pattern of the Winchester surveys of about 1110 and 1148 from memory rather than a table.
